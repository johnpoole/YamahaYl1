# Frame, Chassis & Wheels — Best Practices
## Yamaha YL1

---

## Frame

### Construction
The YL1 uses a conventional tubular steel single-downtube frame. It is not a stressed member — the engine hangs from it rather than being a structural component.

### Inspection procedure
1. Clean all paint and surface rust from suspect areas before inspection. Rust hides cracks.
2. Visually inspect all welds: the steering head, the engine mounts (front and rear), the swing arm pivot, and any gusseted junctions.
3. **Crack detection:** Use a wire brush to clean welds, then apply penetrating dye and developer (dye-penetrant testing) for a definitive crack check. Any cracked weld requires professional repair before the bike is ridden.
4. Check the steering head tube (headstock) for ovalizing or deformation — a previous collision may have bent or cracked it.
5. Check frame alignment: lay the bike on a flat surface (or use a known-flat table) and sight down the centerline. The rear wheel should track directly behind the front.

### Rust treatment
- **Surface rust (cosmetic):** Wire brush, treat with phosphoric acid (rust converter), prime and paint.
- **Pitting rust (under surface):** Same procedure; evaluate depth. Pitting > 1mm in a structural weld area must be assessed by a welder.
- **Through rust:** Frame section must be replaced or professionally repaired. Do not paint over through-rust.

### Paint
- Strip to bare metal before painting structural areas — paint over rust or old paint is not restoration.
- Use a self-etching primer on bare steel before topcoat.
- Frame should match the original YL1 color specification (refer to factory records or model-specific resources) unless the owner is building a custom restoration.

## Front Forks

### Type
The YL1 uses conventional (non-inverted) telescopic forks with hydraulic damping and springs.

### Fluid change (always required on a rebuild)
1. Compress the fork and drain through the drain bolt at the bottom of each leg.
2. Pump the fork several times to expel residual oil.
3. Measure the new oil volume precisely — overfilling reduces travel; underfilling reduces damping.
4. **Recommended oil:** **SAE 30 motor oil, 130cc per leg** *(confirmed: Intertec manual p.13 — "130cc of SAE 30 motor oil each")*. Drain interval: every 4,000 miles.
5. Fill both legs to **identical** levels — unequal damping causes handling issues.

### Inspection
- Check fork tubes for scoring, bending, or pitting. Any scoring on the sliding surface will destroy fork seals immediately.
- Check fork seals for weeping — oil streaks on the tube below the seal indicate seal replacement needed.
- Check the upper clamps (triple tree) for cracks, particularly at the pinch bolt slots.
- Check for bending by removing the forks and rolling each leg on a known-flat surface.

### Seal replacement
- A fork seal driver (or correctly sized socket) is needed to install new seals squarely.
- Install the seal lip side down (inward — toward the oil).
- Lightly coat the seal lip with fork oil before installation.

## Rear Suspension

### Type
Twin shock absorbers (one each side) with spring preload adjustability.

### Inspection
- Check each shock for leaking (oil film on the shaft = damper failure).
- Check spring condition and free length — compressed, sagging springs reduce ground clearance and handling.
- Check the upper and lower pivot bushes/bearings for wear.
- Replace shocks as a **matched pair** — never replace only one.

### Rebuild vs. replace
NOS or quality reproduction shocks for the YL1 vintage are available from vintage Japanese parts suppliers. Rebuilding original shocks is possible but requires disassembly tooling and a source of new seals; replacement is often more practical.

## Wheels & Tires

### Rims
- Inspect for cracks, dents, and flat spots.
- Check spoke tension (all spokes should ring at a consistent pitch when tapped; a dull thud = loose spoke).
- True the wheel by adjusting spoke tension in small increments — use a spoke nipple wrench. Never tighten all spokes on one side, which will pull the rim laterally.
- After truing, check runout at the rim bed: radial (up/down) and lateral (side-to-side) runout should be < 1.5mm for road use.

### Hubs
- Inspect wheel bearings for roughness and play. Replace if any roughness detected.
- Inspect brake drum surface (see Brakes section below) for scoring.

### Tires
Old tires are a safety hazard regardless of tread depth. Rubber hardens and cracks with age, dramatically increasing blow-out risk.
- **Replace any tire over 6–8 years old** regardless of apparent condition.
- YL-1 tire spec: **2.50 × 17** both front and rear *(confirmed: Intertec manual p.11 — "2.50 x17")*. Previous estimate of 2.50-18 / 2.75-18 was incorrect.
- Vintage-profile tires are available from Shinko, Metzeler, and Avon — match the size and rim diameter exactly.

### Tire pressures (confirmed: Intertec manual p.11)
| Wheel | Pressure |
|---|---|
| Front | **1.5 kg/cm² (22 psi)** |
| Rear | **1.9 kg/cm² (28 psi)** |

> Cold pressures. Check before each ride with a quality gauge.

## Brakes

### Type
The YL1 uses drum brakes front and rear (no disc).

### Inspection
- **Brake shoes:** Measure lining thickness. Replace when lining is worn to the rivet heads or below 2mm.
- **Brake drum:** Inspect for scoring (grooves) and measure drum ID. Maximum drum wear diameter varies — check the service manual. A scored drum must be turned or replaced.
- **Brake springs:** Replace if stretched, kinked, or corroded. Weak springs cause dragging and poor return.
- **Brake cam and lever:** Inspect the cam contact pads on the shoes for wear. Inspect the cam itself for wear flats.

### Adjustment
- Brake adjustment is via the adjuster nut on the brake rod/cable.
- Adjust for 20–30mm of free play at the lever before braking begins.
- After adjustment, verify the wheel spins freely with the brake released — brake drag causes overheating and premature wear.

## Fluid Capacities (Drivetrain)

| System | Fluid | Capacity | Drain interval | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Front forks | SAE 30 motor oil | **130 cc per leg** | Every 4,000 miles | Intertec p.13 ✅ |
| Gearbox / transmission | SAE 30 motor oil | **750 cc** | Every 2,000 miles | Intertec p.12 ✅ |

> Use plain SAE 30 motor oil for both systems — **not SAE 10W** fork oil (original erroneous assumption) and not ATF.

## Fasteners

### Corrosion
After 50+ years, most fasteners will be corroded. Soak in penetrating oil (Kroil or AeroKroil) and allow 24–48 hours before attempting removal of suspect fasteners.

### Re-use policy
| Fastener | Condition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Frame bolts, M8 and above | Not corroded, threads good | Re-use (apply anti-seize) |
| Engine bolts into aluminum | Any doubt | Replace with new JIS/metric |
| Nuts with rounded flats | Any condition | Replace |
| Bolts with damaged threads | Any condition | Replace |
| Self-tapping screws (fenders, covers) | Any condition | Replace if worn |

Use new metric hardware to original torque specs. Grade 8.8 or equivalent for all structural applications.
